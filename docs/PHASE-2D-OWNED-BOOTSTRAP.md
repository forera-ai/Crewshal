## Delivered source and unchanged admission

[Owned C helper source](../src/crewshal/bootstrap_helper.c) and [coordinator handoff source](../src/crewshal/linux_bootstrap.py) prepare a narrow same-PID helper-to-native exec route. The C file is packaged as source data, not compiled or installed as an executable. Passive preparation/readback binds its source, an optional explicitly supplied binary digest, exact dispatch and per-session envelope. Even complete prospective hashes leave `bootstrap_implemented`, execution, spend and qualification false. [Dispatch](../src/crewshal/dispatch.py) now binds **sixteen files**, including both new sources. Prior fourteen-source configurations and v18 eligibility cannot transfer.

The proposed helper is a direct retained `subprocess.Popen` child in an already established trusted namespace/role/cgroup envelope. Its own initial exec completes Popen's errpipe handshake before it calls `PTRACE_TRACEME` and stops. No Python `preexec_fn` is used. After verifying that stopped helper's PID/start/parent, executable, argv/environment, namespace, stdio-only pipes and exact worker population, the controller enables exec events and tracer-exit killing, then permits only the owned helper to exec the pinned native image. It requires the exact exec event and unchanged thread/PID identity, and independently rereads the native executable/configuration/pipes while traced.

The handoff does not detach with a stop signal at the exec event. It queues a real SIGSTOP through the retained pidfd, continues to the signal-delivery stop, verifies SIGSTOP and its sender with `PTRACE_GETSIGINFO`, then detaches injecting SIGSTOP. It waits for actual `T` and zero tracer, and calls the **unchanged** `admit_native_process`. Native release and task delivery are absent. A traced `t`, pre-exec helper image, plain post-start SIGSTOP, event-shaped status without delivery verification, wrapper handle or extra worker PID cannot supply admission.

This sequence is a compatibility hypothesis with executable source, not an observed pre-instruction checkpoint. Linux's [ptrace documentation](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/ptrace.2.html) distinguishes exec events, signal-delivery stops, group stops and detach; signals supplied at other stops may be ignored. [Linux v6.8 ptrace source](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v6.8/kernel/ptrace.c) and [signal source](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v6.8/kernel/signal.c) informed the bounded handoff. They are reference material, not the current target's qualification or a claim that source inspection proves scheduling behavior.

## Independent deadline source and owned refusal

The C helper's separate watchdog mode requires retained descriptors: FD 3 is the independently verified owned worker `cgroup.kill` interface, FD 4 reads the parent's lifeline, and FD 5 writes readiness. It arms an absolute `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` timerfd for exactly **five seconds** before publishing its original origin/expiry. Expiry, parent loss, unexpected lifeline input, readiness failure or observer error stop only the supplied worker interface. Closing the lifeline kills; no disarm or deadline reset exists. The future launcher must observe readiness **before creating the worker**, keep the watchdog in the bounded supervisor role outside the worker, and retain independent effective timer/FD/role identity readback.

`ArmedDeadline` parses only a bounded exact acknowledgement, retains the original nanosecond origin/expiry, configuration, worker and lifeline identity, and checks watchdog liveness and deadline around the handoff. Its acknowledgement and Popen object are trusted seam inputs; they **do not prove** the binary, role, timer, kill interface, placement or launch ordering. No current launcher creates or independently observes those resources. Cooperative Python checks alone are not hard enforcement.

After matching retained cleanup ownership, a refusal writes only the pinned worker stop interface and separately observes population and child reaping within the original one-second grace. Stop failure and incomplete recovery remain explicit in `BootstrapRefusal.recovery`. Wrong supplied ownership gives no recovery authority. No numeric-PID kill, automatic retry, path-based deletion, unmount or broad cleanup is added. A reported empty worker does not prove all resources cleaned: retain the watchdog, lifeline and owned resource identities until separately observed terminal cleanup, and never reuse its cgroup while that watchdog survives.
